How a mission runs

From an idea to verified work.

Five steps, in one command center that runs next to your code.

01

Describe the mission

Say what you want to build and what done looks like. Start it in the command center or in your agent's chat. MILO asks a few questions, then lays out the work in order.

  • Questions, decisions and building in one map
  • Plan only, or plan and build
  • Kept in your repo, or in GitHub Issues and Linear
02

It learns your project first

Before anything is built, an agent looks at what you already have and writes a short brief: what exists, what could break and what it's assuming. You approve it, or ask for changes.

  • Nothing gets built until you approve the brief
  • Your product's rules and users, not just the code
  • What it learns is kept for every task after
03

Your agents take what's ready

Agents pick up the work that's ready and work side by side. Each one gets what your project already knows, so you never explain the same thing twice.

  • Each agent can work in its own copy of your project
  • If an agent goes quiet, its work goes back in line
  • When the facts change, they propose a new plan for you to approve
04

You make the calls that matter

Everything that needs you waits in one list, most important first, with suggested answers. Reply in a click, or from your agent's chat with /milo inbox.

  • Only urgent questions interrupt you
  • You decide which calls are always yours
  • Small ones are made and written down for you to review
05

Done means proven, and on the record

You agree on what done means before work starts. Every point needs proof: your tests, a second agent's review, or your sign-off. Every decision is written down with the reasoning.

  • Your product's rules become points to prove
  • Claude and Codex review each other's work
  • A report on how it went: time, your input, rework and cost

Questions

Questions, answered.

What is MILOOPS?
A command center for planning and finishing big projects with AI coding agents. It runs on your computer and shows every mission, ticket, agent and open question in one window.
Who is MILO?
MILO, the Mission Intelligence & Logistics Operator, is your sidekick in the command center. It hands out work, relays agents' questions to you and briefs you on the mission. It never answers on your behalf.
Which agents does it work with?
Any AI coding agent that connects through MCP. Claude Code and Codex have first-class support: one command connects them and adds the /milo command to their chat.
Can I stay in my agent's chat?
Yes. /milo new turns an idea into a mission, /milo resume picks up the work, /milo status tells you where things stand, and /milo inbox lets you answer waiting questions in the chat. The command center shows it all live.
Does my data leave my machine?
MILOOPS keeps your plans in your project folder. If you connect GitHub or Linear, your computer talks to them directly and your keys stay on your computer. Your agents still use their own AI services.
Can I set it up without the command center?
Yes. Everything MILOOPS reads is a plain file in your repo or a command in your terminal: checks, what's always yours, interruptions, workspaces and trackers. The docs cover every setting, with examples.
Can an agent make a decision for me?
Only small, everyday ones, and each is written down so you can review it or change your mind. You decide what always comes to you: by default, anything that changes your data, security, billing or your public API.
Does MILOOPS run my agents for me?
Not on your machine. You start Claude Code or Codex as usual, and they connect to MILOOPS. If you want agents that keep working in the background while you're away, that's MILOOPS Cloud, coming soon.
Can several agents work at the same time?
Yes. Each one can get its own copy of your project on its own branch, and you hear about it before two agents change the same files. If they do, MILOOPS suggests a ticket to bring the work together. It never merges anything for you.
What if the plan turns out to be wrong?
An agent that finds a wrong assumption proposes a new plan, with the evidence and a before-and-after on the map. The work it affects pauses, and nothing changes until you approve. If the work already exists or the mission aims at the wrong problem, an agent can say so and suggest a better goal.
Does it understand my project?
Yes, and it keeps learning. Before building, an agent studies your code and asks about your product, then writes a brief you approve. What agents learn along the way is saved, so the next task starts where the last one left off.
How do I know the work is really done?
Before an agent starts, it writes down what has to be true, and you can confirm it. When it finishes, every point needs proof from your tests, a review by another agent, or your own sign-off. Anything without proof goes back.
Is it free?
Yes. MILOOPS is free to use and runs on your computer.
How do I install it?
Run npx miloops-cli in your project folder (Node.js 20 or newer). Then connect your agent with npx miloops-cli connect claude or npx miloops-cli connect codex. Pick Demo in setup to look around a ready-made mission first.
